## Latest critical prior-claim audit

### EGFLAM: direct prior human target nomination

PMID 26887996, published November 2015, describes three Chinese families with
Meniere disease. Eight patients overall had postlingual sensorineural hearing
loss, but genetic testing and the EGFLAM nomination concern **two members of
one family, 1007184**, not all three families. The primary abstract identifies
EGFLAM c.2057G>A and ITGA8 c.1961C>T as two candidate variants. This is a direct
gene/variant nomination in a human target disorder, even though causality was
unresolved and an alternative candidate remained. Under the protocol's
first-proposed-target rule, exclude EGFLAM from A/B and retain the 2025 retinal
report as expansion/maturation of an earlier target candidate. Do not describe
the 2015 association as established.

This **withdraws my earlier strong-novel-target recommendation** based on the
2025 retinal study alone. That study still supplies three retinal patients in
two unrelated families, with earlier Egflam retinal knockout evidence; clinical
strength does not repair the novelty exclusion.
[2015 primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26887996/),
[2025 retinal report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12679428/).

### DAP3: direct thesis claim, public-release boundary unresolved

Leigh Demain's thesis, awarded 28 November 2017, explicitly proposes DAP3 as a
Perrault syndrome gene. Its institutional abstract describes a proband with
DAP3 p.Cys395Tyr in trans with a large deletion and discusses a possible
non-ribosomal mechanism after observing no mitochondrial translation defect.
This is a direct human hearing/Perrault causal nomination, not a generic
functional mention. The same abstract also nominates **PRORP** and **NOP14**;
PRORP therefore needs the same temporal check if otherwise accepted.

However, award/submission date is not proof of public availability. Europe PMC
ETH 799252 reports firstPublicationDate/dateOfCreation 2017-01-01 but
**firstIndexDate 2024-08-02**. The January 1 date is plainly year-level metadata,
not a verified publication day. No pre-2021 repository release or archived
snapshot was verified in this audit; archive availability requests returned
HTTP 429. Treat DAP3 as **temporal-unresolved pending release evidence**, rather
than assert a proven first public report in November 2017. A verified public
pre-cutoff thesis/abstract would exclude it from A/B; an embargo extending past
the cutoff could change that decision.
[Institutional thesis record and abstract](https://research.manchester.ac.uk/en/studentTheses/identifying-genetic-variants-implicated-in-perrault-syndrome-for-/),
[thesis file](https://pure.manchester.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/156330632/FULL_TEXT.PDF).

### TBX2: regional hearing evidence is clear; gene-specific prior claim unresolved

PMID 22052739, DOI 10.1002/ajmg.a.34302, online 3 November 2011, reports two
17q23.2-deletion patients with sensorineural hearing loss and notes hearing loss
in five of ten accumulated regional-deletion cases. The abstract proposes a
hearing gene somewhere in the interval and reports **no TBX2 sequence variants
in 41 screened hearing-loss patients**. This proves prior human interval
evidence and gene-specific investigation, but does not, by itself, establish
a positive TBX2-specific causal claim. Publisher full-text/PDF requests were
unavailable (HTTP 403), so the precise discussion wording was not verified.

The earlier Ballif 2010 full text (PMID 20206336, PMC2833380) nominates TBX2/TBX4
for parts of the deletion syndrome, with its explicit TBX2 phenotype discussion
focused on digits/limbs. Nimmakayalu 2011 (PMID 21271665) reports hearing loss
with a larger deletion, while its abstract specifically proposes TBX2/TBX4
investigation for pulmonary hypertension. Neither accessible text closes the
hearing-specific attribution question.

Therefore withdraw the earlier **unqualified** A/B recommendation and mark
**prior-regional-candidate / gene-specific novelty unresolved**. Do not equate
every gene in a hearing-loss CNV with a prior disease-gene proposal. If the
2011 discussion explicitly nominates TBX2 as causal for the human hearing
phenotype, the first-proposed rule excludes it; if it only documents a negative
candidate screen, the 2024 frameshift report remains a plausible first positive
gene-specific claim. The latter has two unrelated families, segregation and
functional support; preprint 19 July 2024, full paper 17 September 2025.
[2011 primary abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ajmg.a.34302),
[2010 primary full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2833380/),
[2025 gene-specific study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12772546/).

## Ascertainment and protocol findings retained from earlier reviews

- A ledger is required for every catalog gene/domain. Historical green status
  can close first-target eligibility only when it represents dated human
  clinical target evidence; historical absence does not establish novelty.
- Review red/no-reference cases and late references even when older functional
  references exist. Earliest-cited-publication screening alone misses new human
  associations. Gene/domain changes and unresolved records must stay visible.
- Annotated and compound PMID strings were omitted by the initial parser.
  Examples included B9D1, HYLS1, B9D2 and DIAPH1. Resolved nonstandard references
  must be linked back to genes, not merely fetched as an unassociated list.
- Queries requiring both human and variant words miss clinical reports.
  Include heterotaxy/laterality and historical aliases; record handling for
  ambiguous short aliases. Merge every pagination page and preserve page hashes.
- The main novelty endpoint combines first-any-human-target and first-target
  after non-target disease, with separate reporting. Pre-cutoff target
  candidates remain prior proposals even if evidence strengthens later.
- Use gene-specific target-family counts. Independent replication must add a
  new target family. Healthy localization and in-silico predictions alone do
  not satisfy functional support. Earlier target-relevant perturbation can
  qualify if cited separately; this rule was applied to EGFLAM/SAXO6 equally.
- Freeze handling of disputed associations and uncertain public dates before
  ranking. Thesis award dates, acceptance dates and issue dates are not
  interchangeable with first public availability.
- Retain all qualified/in-universe/scored denominators, absent identifiers,
  NULL source values and ties. Original HIGH threshold is three; a threshold
  of four is a named sensitivity, not an undisclosed replacement.
- Unknown-only animal ontology rows are NULL, not negative evidence. Record
  native ortholog multiplicity; uniform scaling is not mapping robustness.
- Date-filtered present-day abstracts cannot recreate historical text. Preserve
  the no-literature historical-source analysis alongside the augmented one.
- The disclosed Menon 2019 photoreceptor substitute and platform/no-photo
  sensitivities were acceptable as a historical analogue; matrix arithmetic
  was not independently audited in these clinical-review rounds.

All recommendations are pre-outcome. No numerical result motivated inclusion,
exclusion, or a change of evidence threshold.

## Final modifier, segregation, and publication-date follow-up

This follow-up examined the 45-row strong draft without reading scores or
outcomes. The parent owns the roster and its final adjudications; only this
review document was edited by this reviewer.

- **SAMD7:** The original [2016 primary report, PMID26887858](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4758057/)
  was published on 2016-02-18 (DOI10.1038/srep21307). It explicitly proposes a
  modifying contribution of noncoding SAMD7 variants to the unusual retinal
  phenotype of two siblings carrying RHO p.Glu150Lys. Reporter and retinal
  explant experiments support the modifier claim. Under the same rule that
  counts prior explicit human candidate/modifier associations, this is prior
  target evidence and excludes SAMD7 from first-target A/B. It is not merely
  a co-mention of two genes.
- **LETM1:** The accessible [2019 original abstract, PMID30773800](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30773800/)
  (DOI10.1002/ajmg.a.61092; online 2019-02-17) reports retinal dystrophy and
  sensorineural deafness with homozygous OTX2, then nominates LETM1 and other
  variants for ataxia, myopathy and respiratory-chain abnormalities not
  explained by OTX2. The abstract does not explicitly assign the sensory
  abnormalities to LETM1. The [2022 primary cohort](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9502063/)
  identifies that same person as family 8, but this retrospective inclusion
  cannot establish what the 2019 report originally claimed about sensory
  causality. Full original 2019 text was unavailable through the publisher or
  restricted institutional PDF. Thus, excluding LETM1 solely because the
  gene and sensory findings co-occur in that abstract is unsupported under
  the parent's clarified rule. A prior nontarget candidate claim is supported;
  B remains plausible, with original target attribution unresolved.
- **MIEF1:** The [2021 optic-neuropathy primary report, PMID33632269](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7905578/)
  was published on 2021-02-25 (DOI10.1186/s13024-021-00431-w). It reports two
  independent women, with distinct heterozygous p.Tyr240Asn and p.Arg146Trp
  variants, plus live-cell mitochondrial fusion defects. Full text provides
  no pedigrees, relative testing, family history or segregation. Two
  independent probands must not be described as two segregating dominant
  pedigrees. If the frozen inheritance criterion requires family segregation
  or established de novo status, keep candidate pending additional evidence.
  The earlier [Parkinson candidate preprint](https://doi.org/10.1101/2020.11.23.20235671),
  PPR243603, was first posted 2020-11-25 and is a nontarget human candidate
  claim. If MIEF1 is retained, this supports B rather than A.

fields still set to null in the inspected draft require populated evidence
and source references before freeze; prose mentioning functional work alone
does not make a completed structured adjudication. PDIA6, TMEM72 and PSKH1
had empty domain arrays despite their ciliary clinical-domain descriptions.
This was a targeted final consistency screen, not a new exhaustive discovery
search or an independent numerical-analysis audit.

## Pre-freeze reconstruction and evaluator review

The parent subsequently requested a pre-outcome code review of
`scripts/revision_temporal_v2_reconstruct.py`,
`scripts/revision_temporal_v2_evaluate.py`, their imported transform helpers,
the protocol and the revised roster. No reconstruction, evaluator or source
transform was executed on real data. Synthetic arrays only were used to check
rounding, ranks, missingness and weight arithmetic. This section therefore
supersedes the preceding statement about not auditing numerical code, but is
not evidence that the real-data outputs have been run or verified.

### Concrete findings and resolution observed

1. **Q75 interpolation mismatch, fixed before outcomes.** The original evaluator
   used NumPy `quantile(..., method='nearest')` while claiming production Polars
   nearest parity. On the synthetic positive array 1 through 7, NumPy returns
   5 whereas Polars returns 6; on 1 through 15, the values are 11 and 12.
   This can change the HIGH gate. The parent changed the evaluator to actual
   `pl.Series(positive).quantile(.75, interpolation='nearest')` and clarified
   the protocol. The corrected implementation was re-read.
2. **Inflated mapped-animal audit counts, fixed.** Looking up every historical
   universe gene with `mgi_links[gid]` and `zlinks[gid]` inserted empty keys into
   both defaultdicts, so subsequent `len(...)` counts falsely implied universal
   ortholog coverage. The parent changed these lookups to `.get(gid, set())`;
   the corrected implementation was re-read. This was an audit-field error,
   not a demonstrated change to the score vector.
3. **TopK comparison support mismatch, clarified before freeze.** The code
   calculates Spearman correlation on shared scored genes but takes each
   scheme's topK from its complete scored ranking. Earlier protocol wording
   said both were on shared support. The parent revised the protocol to state
   the implemented complete-ranking topK comparison, with coverage and shared
   counts. This retains missing-coverage effects and is an acceptable
   prespecified comparison; it is not the same as reranking only common genes.

### Arithmetic and source semantics assessed

- Weighted means redistribute weights over finite observed components and keep
  observed zero in the denominator. All-missing rows remain missing. Evidence
  count includes only observed components whose scheme weight is positive.
- Deterministic positions use descending score and ascending Ensembl ID;
  statistical tie minimum/maximum ranks and average percentiles are separate.
  The conservative topK rule requires the whole tie to fit within K; missing
  and out-of-universe cases fail every recovery rule.
- Main historical-universe denominators retain eligible missing-score genes;
  all-eligible and scored-subset denominators are additionally reported. Genes
  are deduplicated by clinical HGNC key. The noncoding principal cases remain
  in the end-to-end denominator even if absent from the protein-coding universe.
- The expression transform ranks positive observations only, sets measured
  zero to zero and preserves missing values. Synthetic photoreceptor inputs
  `[0, 1, 3, NULL]` produced `[0, .5, 1, NULL]`. It invokes the production
  source-separated expression transform, avoiding mixing HPA ordinal codes
  with raw TPM/count values.
- Literature scoring follows its own production rule: positive raw scores use
  average rank over all observed raw values, while zero remains zero. This is
  distinct from expression's positive-only rank denominator. Missing Entrez
  mapping remains NULL; a mapped gene with no archived linked PMID has explicit
  zero before division. That no-link behavior is an expressly documented
  historical source rule, not evidence of no publications anywhere.
- Animal unknown-only/no-recognized-positive channels remain NULL; known
  nonrelevant terms can establish observed zero. Partial recognized positives
  are reported as lower bounds when unresolved labels remain. The native-link
  and unique-link variants use the frozen historical mappings, not selected
  modern orthologs. The uniform scaling variants are not described as ortholog
  choice robustness.
- GO-only annotation intentionally retains the production 0.5 GO contribution
  with missing UniProt/pathway channels; the available-GO rescale is separate.
  No hidden replacement with an all-available-source normalization was found.
- Synthetic weighted-mean, deterministic-tie and NULL-recovery checks also
  passed. These checks were performed in the local `.venv` and read no real
  evidence, ranking, or outcome tables.

### Requirements for a defensible final freeze

The reviewed `verified_freeze` function checks the files listed in the manifest,
but does not itself require that every dependency be listed. The final manifest
must cover the final clinical roster and protocol, every consumed inventory
source, and imported scoring code/constants/helpers in addition to the two
entry scripts. Relevant dependencies include `revision_phase2.py`,
`revision_temporal_analysis.py`, annotation/expression/localization transforms
and models, animal keyword models, and the dependency lockfile. Otherwise a
manifest can verify successfully while an omitted runtime dependency changes.
The committed final manifest had not yet been presented for this review.

The evaluator retains direct-localization and animal gate routes when a
scheme gives one of those evidence families zero score weight. This is a
**score-weight omission with a fixed gate**, not complete removal of that
source from HIGH classification. The final protocol/report must name this
semantics explicitly, or change and freeze the gate-omission design before
outcomes. Single-family HIGH is necessarily unavailable under a three-family
minimum; interpret its rank comparison separately from HIGH recovery.

At the latest roster inspection there were 45 principal genes and 1,092 ledger
records. All 45 now had nonempty functional/replication support fields and
domain arrays. The table still contained 358 historical green records and 96
RetNet records requiring target confirmation, plus 441 records without an
accepted postcutoff nomination. Their selection disposition can be frozen
without pretending their clinical truth is fully resolved. A closed selection
decision should coexist with unresolved/screened clinical-evidence status.
These records are not negative controls, and the study must not claim complete
clinical adjudication or exhaustive discovery coverage of the entire catalog.
Conditional recovery of the frozen 45-gene cohort is still evaluable, with the
ascertainment limitations and counts stated before outcomes.

The final clinical decisions supplied by the parent retain LETM1, TBX2 and
DAP3 as excluded unresolved records; SAMD7 has a prior target modifier claim;
MIEF1, OGDHL, GPN2, PLXNB2 and RNU6-1 remain separate candidates. This review
found no reason to merge those candidates into the principal denominator.
After the Q75 correction and audit/protocol fixes above, no additional fatal
principal-cohort arithmetic error was identified in the reviewed code. This
conclusion remains conditional on final freeze completeness and the named
gate semantics; it does not certify yet-unexecuted real-data results.
